Something takes off. A clip, a snippet, a post you did not think about much. The numbers climb for about two days and then they go back to normal, and a month later you cannot point at anything that changed.

That is the usual outcome, and it is not bad luck. A spike hands you attention on loan. What you do in the window decides whether any of it becomes yours.

## The 48 hours that decide it {#the-48-hours}

The mistake is treating a spike as a result. It is a queue of strangers, and queues disperse.

So the first job is a destination. Before you do anything clever, make sure the link in your bio goes somewhere that asks for one thing. Not a menu of nine links. One page, one ask: the song, or the list, or the show. A person who arrived from a fifteen second video will make exactly one decision before they leave.

The second job is to collect something you keep. A follow is a lease on someone's attention that the platform can revoke by changing what it shows them. An email address, a phone number, a Discord invite, a Beacons subscriber: those survive a distribution change. Ask for it while the traffic is live, not on Monday when you have time.

I would rather have four hundred emails from a spike than forty thousand views from it, and that is not a modest position. Four hundred people you can reach directly will show up to a show, pre-save a record and buy a shirt. Forty thousand views will not do any of those things by themselves.

## Send it somewhere that converts {#send-it-somewhere}

Not all attention is worth the same, and the gap is bigger than people expect. Short-form video platforms tend to move people to streaming better than a feed post does, because the sound is already the thing they engaged with. A photo post asks the viewer to go and look for the music. A video with your track under it has already played it to them.

  <p>You will see figures claiming one platform converts ten times better than another. I have not found a source for that number I would put my name on, and account-level conversion varies enormously by genre and by how the link is set up. Treat the direction as sound and the multiple as unproven. Measure your own: unique link clicks divided by views, per platform, for a month.</p>

The measurement matters more than the benchmark. Set up a separate link for each platform so you can tell them apart, then look at click rate rather than view count. Most artists discover their smaller platform is doing more work than the big one, and then move their effort accordingly.

The wider context is worth holding in your head while you do this. [Luminate's 2026 midyear report](https://luminatedata.com/reports/) put global on-demand streams up 9.8 percent, to 2.8 trillion, while US growth reached only 4.8 percent. More listening is happening than ever, and it is spread across more releases than ever. Attention is abundant. The scarce thing is a way to reach the same person twice.

## Do not redecorate the museum {#do-not-redecorate}

Here is where most spikes get wasted, and it looks like enthusiasm.

Something works, so you make five more of it. The feed fills with variations on the one post that popped, and the act you were building gets buried under the thing that happened to it. Six weeks later the spike is gone and so is the through line.

Think of your profile as a room somebody walks into. They found one piece, they liked it, now they are looking around to decide whether to stay. What they need to see is a body of work that makes sense. A wall of near-identical reactions to a single moment tells them there is nothing here except the moment.

Post again fast, yes. Post the thing you were already making. If the viral clip genuinely revealed something about what people want from you, that is real information, and you fold it into the next month of work rather than into the next four days of panic.

## What the week should leave behind {#what-it-leaves}

Judge a spike by what is still there in thirty days:

Emails or numbers collected, with a name attached. Pre-saves or follows on the platform where you actually release. One new piece of work made while people were paying attention. And a note in your own file about which link converted and which did not, because you will spike again and next time you will start from evidence.

Everything else was weather.

**Your move.** Fix the link in your bio today, before anything takes off. It costs twenty minutes and it is the only part of this you cannot do retroactively.
