The reason most artists post twice a week and then stop is not laziness. It is that every post starts from zero: find the idea, set up the phone, get the light right, record eight takes, hate all of them.

Do that thirty times a month and you have spent your month on setup.

## One setup, thirty pieces {#one-setup}

The fix is boring and it works. Pick one hour. Set the phone up once. Film everything.

What makes the hour work is deciding the shape before you press record. You are not filming thirty ideas, you are filming three formats about ten times each:

The playing clip. You, the instrument, thirty seconds of the part of the song that actually hooks. No talking.

The talking clip. One sentence about how something got made, said to camera, no intro. Fifteen seconds.

The proof clip. Something that happened. A rehearsal, a mistake, a message from someone who heard it, a session file with forty tracks in it.

Three formats and one setup means you never make a creative decision and a technical decision at the same time, which is the thing that eats afternoons. Change your shirt three times across the hour and swap between two spots in the room, and the month does not look like it was filmed in one go.

## Change one thing at a time {#change-one-thing}

Here is the part people skip.

The same artist will get 300 views on one post and 300,000 on another and conclude that the algorithm is random. It usually is not. It is that the two posts differed in six ways at once, so nothing can be learned from either.

Post the next one with **one** deliberate difference. Same clip, different first line. Or same first line, different opening frame. Or same everything, posted at a different hour. One variable, then look at the number.

You need maybe five or six of these before a pattern shows up, and the pattern is almost never the thing you expected. In my experience it is the first frame far more often than the caption, and it is almost never the hashtags.

## What the month looks like {#the-month}

One hour of filming, thirty raw clips, then perhaps two hours of editing spread across the month. Posting becomes a five-minute job rather than a creative crisis.

The real gain is not the time. It is that you stop deciding whether to post today, because today's post was decided three weeks ago. Deciding is what exhausts people, not filming.

**Your move.** Block one hour this week and film the three formats ten times each. Do not edit anything during that hour. Editing during filming is how one hour becomes five.
